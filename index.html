ignore

/ɪɡˈnɔːr/ verb

To pay no attention to someone or something, whether by choice or by oversight.

Updated October 24, 2026 · 5 minute read

We all ignore things in writing: warnings, rules, rumors, even people. The trouble is that repeating the same verb makes sentences feel flat, and it can blur your meaning. A strong list of Synonyms for Ignore lets you show whether someone missed a detail, chose to dismiss it, or went out of their way to snub it. This guide sorts the options by tone, adds examples, and ends with grammar notes so every choice sounds natural.

The short answer

Best all-round choices: disregard for formal writing, overlook for accidental misses, brush off for casual dismissal, and snub for deliberate rudeness toward a person.

The words below look interchangeable, yet each carries its own shade of intent. Pick by asking two questions: was it deliberate, and was it a thing or a person?

Synonyms for ignore, grouped by meaning

1. Formal and neutral

Suited to reports, essays and professional emails.

  • disregard
  • overlook
  • neglect
  • pay no heed to
  • take no notice of
  • set aside

The committee chose to disregard the outdated figures.

2. Casual and conversational

Natural in speech, messages and relaxed writing.

  • brush off
  • blow off
  • tune out
  • shrug off
  • wave away
  • skip over

He tried to brush off the criticism and keep working.

3. Deliberately rude toward a person

Use when the slight is intentional and social.

  • snub
  • shun
  • cold-shoulder
  • rebuff
  • cut dead
  • give the silent treatment

After the argument, she snubbed him at every meeting.

4. Knowingly looking away

Describes seeing a problem and choosing not to act.

  • turn a blind eye to
  • look the other way
  • gloss over
  • let slide
  • pass over
  • condone

The manager turned a blind eye to the late deliveries.

Ignore, overlook, disregard and neglect

These four are the most confused. Here is how they differ.

Quick comparison
WordCore ideaExample
ignoreChoose not to pay attentionShe ignored the alarm.
overlookFail to notice, or let a fault passI overlooked a typo.
disregardTreat as unimportant, often formallyPlease disregard this notice.
neglectFail to care for or do somethingHe neglected his duties.

Word choice that works and word choice that misleads

Less precise

"I ignored your email by accident." Ignore usually implies a choice, so this sounds odd.

More precise

"I overlooked your email by accident." Overlook fits a genuine miss.

Less precise

"Please snub this message." Snub is for slighting a person, not a document.

More precise

"Please disregard this message." Disregard suits notices and instructions.

Quick grammar notes

  • Ignore is transitive. It needs an object: "They ignored the rule," not "They ignored."
  • Gerunds work too. "He ignored warning signs" or "She ignored hearing the call."
  • Phrasal verbs are informal. Brush off, blow off and tune out suit casual tone more than formal reports.
  • Do not confuse related words. Ignore is the verb, ignorant is an adjective, and ignorance is the noun.
  • Watch your prepositions. Phrases like "pay no attention to" and "turn a blind eye to" need "to" before the object.

Antonyms for ignore

When you want the reverse meaning, choose from these.

  • notice
  • heed
  • acknowledge
  • observe
  • attend to
  • respond to
  • take into account

Frequently asked questions

What is the best synonym for ignore?

It depends on meaning. Disregard fits formal writing, overlook suggests missing something, and snub describes a deliberate slight.

What is the difference between ignore and overlook?

Ignore usually means choosing not to pay attention, while overlook often means failing to notice, though it can also mean letting a fault pass.

Is ignore a transitive verb?

Yes. It needs an object, as in "She ignored the warning."

What is the opposite of ignore?

Common antonyms include notice, heed, acknowledge, attend to and observe.
